## Eindhoven University of Technology

### Quantitative Research Methods and Statistics (7U0B20), 2026/27
*Course coordinator and lecturer, Department of the Built Environment*

Compulsory bachelor course, 5 EC. An introduction to quantitative research design and statistics for the built environment, covering measurement, sampling, descriptive and inferential statistics, correlation, and regression. Applications draw on housing, real estate, and urban questions. I coordinate the course and teach the research methods and statistical analysis modules, working with two co-lecturers and eight teaching assistants.

### Housing, Real Estate and Urban Economics (7EU0B20), 2026/27
*Co-lecturer, Department of the Built Environment*

Bachelor elective on housing systems, residential real estate investment, housing market dynamics, and urban economics. I teach the session on housing choice and preference research.

### Healthy and Sustainable Living Environments (7UB1B10), 2026/27
*Co-lecturer, Department of the Built Environment*

Compulsory bachelor course, 5 EC, on how the design and management of the built environment shape health, well-being, and sustainability. I teach the session on spatial planning and urban economics.

## Freie Universität Berlin

### Introduction to Urban Sociology, 2025/26
*Lecturer, John F. Kennedy Institute*

Cities are fundamental sites of modern social life, concentrating both opportunity and inequality, innovation and conflict. Understanding cities requires examining how markets, states, and social movements shape urban space, and how race, class, gender, and immigration intersect in the urban context.

This seminar examines American cities over the past 150 years through major theoretical traditions and empirical themes. We explore housing markets, racial segregation, immigration, suburbanization, gentrification, policing, gender, finance, education, and urban politics. Throughout, we maintain a comparative lens, juxtaposing American patterns with European experiences.
